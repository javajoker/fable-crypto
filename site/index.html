What stands behind crypto market cap?

A small, open model of one measurable base under the crypto market: stablecoins. They are a general equivalent, not the only one. Fiat buys crypto directly, and stablecoins also pay for things outside crypto. But they are the part that can be counted, and short-term US Treasuries hold their peg, so the model reads the base as locked bonds first and locked dollars second, then asks what multiple the market puts on it. With an Excel workbook, a scenario app, and the tools to keep both current.

The chain today

The wider frame

The Fed sets how much money there is and what it costs, Wall Street sets the appetite for risk, and the Treasury market supplies the bills that hold the peg. The split between locked bonds and locked USD assumes of stablecoin reserves are in Treasuries; try other values in the forecast app.

Keeping it current

  1. Fetch. python3 scripts/fetch_inputs.py "2026 Q4" pulls M2, GDP, CPI, the policy rate, reverse repo, public debt, Treasury bills, stablecoin supply and tokenised RWAs, and writes a file with three values left to fill in by hand.
  2. Apply. python3 scripts/add_quarter.py --from-json data/quarters/2026Q4.json adds the quarter to the workbook and checks it against an independent recomputation.
  3. Publish. make update runs the check again and rebuilds this site's data from the workbook.

make audit compares the workbook with the sources at any time. Details in the toolset guide.

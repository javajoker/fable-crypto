How much crypto market cap can the stablecoin base carry?

Stablecoins are crypto's general equivalent: tokens, and increasingly tokenised real-world assets and asset-backed securities, are priced and settled in them. Their peg is held by short-term US Treasuries, so the base of the market is locked bonds first and locked dollars second. Trading multiplies that base into market cap. Read this first. This is a scenario tool, not a prediction. It turns a view on the Fed, the Treasury and Wall Street into a crypto market cap; it cannot supply the view. How the numbers are made

The base. A stablecoin keeps its peg because the issuer holds short-term US Treasuries against it. So the base has two legs: locked bonds = base × backing share and locked USD = base × (1 − backing share), the cash part of the reserves. Only the cash leg ties up M2 dollars. A dollar that buys a stablecoin is spent by the issuer on a Treasury bill and goes on circulating; what stays locked is the bill. Set the backing share to 100% and locked USD is zero while locked bonds are not, and every number below is computed the same way.

The general equivalent. Inside the crypto market the stablecoin is what everything else is priced and settled in: tokens, and a growing set of tokenised real-world assets (RWAs) and asset-backed securities (ABS). That is why the base can grow faster than either leg: over the last year it grew . Crypto V is market cap ÷ base, the multiple that secondary-market trading puts on each stablecoin dollar, and market cap = V × (locked bonds + locked USD).

Driver model. The bond leg follows public debt (the Treasury), the cash leg follows M2 (the Fed), and growth in use comes on top. Crypto V moves with Wall Street and is pulled back toward its own average: Δln V = β · Δln(US equity cap) + κ · (average ln V − last ln V), with . The band around the scenario path is the model's typical one-quarter error, widened with the square root of the horizon. The V equation was picked as the most stable of about twenty candidate forms, which itself flatters it a little.

The dollar side. Flowing USD is M2. Free USD is the part not needed to turn the real economy over at its normal pace: M2 − GDP ÷ V*, with V* = (the 2015–2019 velocity of M2). It is shown for scale. It does not cap the base, because the base is backed by bonds. The shaded range in the chart uses the middle half of V over the last quarters.

Two meanings of V. Crypto V here is market cap ÷ base, the counterpart of M2 velocity with market value in the place of GDP. Turnover velocity, trading volume ÷ base, is shown when live data is loaded; its history has no free source.

What is left out. Total public debt stands in for the stock of Treasury bills, which is the part issuers actually hold. The market-cap series counts tokenised RWAs and ABS only in part, so as they grow V understates what the base carries. Dollars that bought coins directly, or through ETFs, never became stablecoins and are not in the base. Read V as a ratio, not as a law.

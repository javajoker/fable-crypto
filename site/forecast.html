How much USD can become crypto market cap?

Follow the dollars: of all the USD in circulation, some is free to move, part of that gets locked into the crypto market, and trading multiplies it into market cap. The same locked dollars are lent on to the US government as Treasury collateral. Set each step and see where total crypto and Bitcoin end up.

How the numbers are made

Driver model. Locked USD = lock-in share × M2, so the Fed moves it through the money stock. Crypto V moves with Wall Street and is pulled back toward its own average: Δln V = β · Δln(US equity cap) + κ · (average ln V − last ln V), with . Market cap = V × locked USD. The band around the scenario path is the model's typical one-quarter error, widened with the square root of the horizon. It was picked as the most stable of about twenty candidate forms, which itself flatters it a little.

Liquidity flow. Flowing USD is M2. Free USD is the part not needed to turn the real economy over at its normal pace: M2 − GDP ÷ V*, with V* = (the 2015–2019 velocity of M2). Locked USD is the supply of USD stablecoins: dollars that entered the crypto market through the primary market, by minting, and stay there as dollars. Crypto V is market cap ÷ locked USD, the multiple that secondary-market trading puts on each locked dollar. The scenario sets market cap = V × lock-in share × M2. The shaded range uses the middle half of V over the last quarters.

The dual lens. A dollar that buys a stablecoin does not leave the banking system. The issuer spends it on Treasury bills, so the cash goes to whoever sold the bills and M2 is unchanged. What is created is a second claim: a dollar-token that circulates inside the crypto market, backed by a Treasury bill outside it. Locked USD therefore shows up twice, as liquidity inside crypto and as demand for US government debt. The Treasury figures are locked USD × the backing share you set.

Two meanings of V. Crypto V here is market cap ÷ locked USD, the counterpart of M2 velocity with market value in the place of GDP. Turnover velocity, trading volume ÷ locked USD, is shown when live data is loaded; its history has no free source.

What this measure of locked USD leaves out. Stablecoins are the narrowest count: dollars that bought coins directly, or through ETFs, also entered but are not in it. So V here is larger than the true price impact of a dollar of inflow, and it should be read as a ratio, not as a law.
